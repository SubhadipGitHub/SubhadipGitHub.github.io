Six months of banking controls

The shortest entry here, and the one I would most like to extend. Banking changed my default assumptions about what an integration owes the people running it.

6 mosTime in sector
Controls & reconciliationPrimary focus
ConsultantWorking as

What I worked on

The work was interface and reconciliation focused: moving data between core and downstream systems under controls that assume, correctly, that everyone will eventually need to prove what happened. Every flow had to be able to account for itself — what it read, what it wrote, what it rejected and why — with a trail that would survive an audit rather than a code review.

That also meant a slower, more deliberate change process than I was used to. Segregated duties, controlled promotion, and evidence attached to every release. Frustrating at first, then genuinely instructive: most of the ceremony exists because someone once skipped it.

Systems & platforms

Oracle Integration Cloud Oracle Fusion ERP Secure file transfer Reconciliation controls Audit logging REST APIs Scheduled batch interfaces

Representative work

Controlled batch interfaces

Scheduled interfaces between core and downstream systems, with checksums, control totals and rejection handling designed so a partial run could be proven partial rather than assumed complete.

Reconciliation and exception reporting

Reporting comparing what was sent against what was accepted, with exceptions itemised for the operations team rather than aggregated into a single pass or fail.

Audit-ready logging

Structured logging at the flow level capturing enough detail to reconstruct any given run after the fact, without carrying sensitive payload data into the log store.

What the sector taught me

Six months was enough to permanently raise my baseline. Banking treats “can you prove it?” as a design requirement rather than an afterthought, and I have carried that into every sector I have worked in since. It is the sector I would most like to go deeper in.
